## Summary
Ranch BRRRR, Kohler Ave is a purchase at $112,000 with a $46,000 rehab and an after repair value of $205,000. The headline return is strong, but it depends on the sale at exit. Once refinanced, the rental does not cover its costs and debt.

## Returns
The model shows an IRR of 22.1%, an equity multiple of 4.96x and an annualized return on cash of 17.4%. Total profit over the hold is $116,886. The cap rate on purchase price is 11.7% and the yield on total cost is 8.2%.

Almost all of that profit comes at the end. Payback is 120 months, the full hold. The sale price at exit is $272,802, with net proceeds of $122,349. Operations add little: stabilized net operating income is $13,151, but stabilized cash flow is -$197 for the year, or -$16 a month. Cash on cash return is -1%. The result rests on 3% yearly appreciation holding for ten years.

## Financing
The purchase loan is 85% of cost at 11%. Cash needed at closing is $22,064, and the most cash in the deal at once is $29,315. The refinance comes after 6 months at 75% loan to value and a 7% rate. It returns $10,219 in cash out and leaves $19,393 in the deal.

## Risks
- **Negative cash flow.** The stabilized year loses money every month. Any rent shortfall or repair overrun adds to the loss, and the owner must cover it.
- **Thin debt coverage.** The debt service coverage ratio is 1.07. Most lenders want 1.25 or better, so the refinance may not be approved on these terms.
- **Cash stays tied up.** $19,393 remains in the deal after the refinance, so this is not a full cash-out.
- **Little occupancy cushion.** Break even occupancy is 96.1% against a 5% vacancy allowance.
- **Refinance timing.** The rehab takes 4 months and the refinance is set at 6 months. Delays leave the 11% purchase loan in place longer.
- **Value dependence.** The $205,000 after repair value sets the refinance amount. A lower appraisal means less cash out and more cash left in.

## Bottom line
The projected returns look good, but they come from appreciation and the sale, not from rental income. With a debt service coverage ratio of 1.07 and negative cash flow, I would not approve this as presented. It would need a lower rehab cost, a lower purchase price, higher rent or a smaller refinance loan before the rental carries itself.
