## Summary
This is a subject-to purchase of a fourplex at 3305 S 12th St, Sheboygan, WI, for $310,000. The buyer takes over a $205,000 loan at 3.2% and gives the seller $15,000 in cash plus a seller note at 4.5%. The headline returns look strong, but they depend on a low cash outlay, steady growth, and two financing terms that could break the structure.

## Returns
- Total profit over the 120-month hold: $234,798
- IRR: 38.2%, equity multiple: 3.45x
- Annualized return on cash: 17.9%, payback: 46 months
- Cap rate and yield on total cost: both 7.3%
- Stabilized net operating income: $22,746
- Stabilized cash flow: $3,817 a year, or $318 a month
- Cash on cash return: 21.1%
- Exit sale price: $416,614, with net proceeds of $245,925

The exit assumes 3% annual appreciation and 3% annual rent growth. Much of the profit comes from the sale, not from operating cash flow.

## Financing
- Existing loan: $205,000 taken over at 3.2%
- Cash to seller: $15,000
- Seller note: 4.5%, with an 84-month balloon
- Cash needed at closing: $18,100
- Most cash in the deal at once: $55,807
- Debt service coverage ratio: 1.33

Closing cash is small, but the peak cash exposure is well above it. The investor needs to have that capital available.

## Risks
1. **Balloon.** A $78,324 balloon comes due in month 84. The hold runs 120 months, so the investor must refinance, pay it off, or sell before the balloon date. A weak market or tight credit at that point would hurt.
2. **Due-on-sale.** The seller's lender can call the existing loan when the deed transfers. If it does, the 3.2% financing is gone and the deal would need replacement debt at current terms. Nothing in the figures shows that outcome.
3. **Thin cash flow.** Stabilized cash flow is $318 a month. A debt service coverage ratio of 1.33 and break even occupancy of 82.5% leave limited room beyond the 5% vacancy allowance.
4. **Growth assumptions.** Returns rely on 3% rent growth and 3% appreciation each year. Flat rents or prices would cut the profit sharply.

## Bottom line
The numbers work on paper, and the cash at closing is low. The structure carries real risk, though. The investor should have a plan for a loan call and a funded exit or refinance before month 84, and should treat the 38.2% IRR as dependent on appreciation and on the existing loan staying in place.
