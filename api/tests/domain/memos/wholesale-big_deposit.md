## Summary
This is a wholesale assignment of the Estate sale wholesale property at 1104 Michigan Ave, Sheboygan, WI. The contract price is $96,000 against an after repair value of $198,000. The deal works on paper, but the assignment fee is $2,586, which is thin for the risk carried.

## Returns
- Assignment fee: $2,586, with the most a buyer can pay at $98,586.
- Buyer profit at that price: $29,700, based on a $42,000 rehab estimate and the after repair value of $198,000. This meets the 15% margin the buyer needs.
- Buyer cash needed at closing: $13,605.

## Risks
- **Thin fee (caution flag).** Room for an assignment fee is under $5,000. At $2,586, one small price concession from a buyer erases much of the payoff.
- **Earnest money exposure.** Earnest money at risk is $5,000, which is larger than the $2,586 fee. If no buyer closes, the loss outweighs the best-case gain on this deal.
- **Gap to the 70% rule.** The 70% rule maximum offer is $96,600. The most a buyer can pay, $98,586, is above that figure. Buyers who hold to the 70% rule will see the assigned price as too high and may push back or pass.
- **Rehab sensitivity.** The $42,000 rehab estimate is the biggest swing factor. If a buyer's scope runs higher, they will want the price cut, and the fee is the only place that cut can come from.
- **Value assumption.** The $198,000 after repair value drives everything. If comparable sales support less, the buyer's margin shrinks and the fee may not be achievable.
- **Estate seller.** Estate sales can bring probate or title timing issues that delay closing, which strains a deal with this little cushion.

## Bottom line
The numbers hold together, but the margin for the wholesaler is small compared with the $5,000 at risk. I would proceed only if a buyer is already lined up, the after repair value and rehab scope are confirmed, and the contract lets you exit cheaply if the estate cannot deliver clean title on time. Otherwise, try to renegotiate the contract price down to widen the fee.
