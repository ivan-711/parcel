## Summary
This is a fix and flip of a Cape Cod at 915 Erie Ave, Sheboygan, bought at $139,000 with a $38,000 rehab and an after repair value of $232,000. The deal shows a profit of $21,729, but the margin is thin against the resale price, so the numbers hold up only if the budget and the sale price do.

## Returns
Total profit over the hold is $21,729. The equity multiple is 1.82x, and payback comes in 5 months. IRR is 412.6% and annualized return on cash is 319.4%. Those two percentages look large because the hold is short and the cash invested is small. They show how fast the money turns over, not how much cushion the deal has. Sale price at exit is $232,000, with selling costs at 8%, leaving net proceeds of $50,340.

## Financing
The loan covers 90% of cost at an 11% interest rate with 2% in points. Cash needed at closing is $19,182. The most cash in the deal at once is $26,586, so reserves should be sized to that peak, not to the closing figure alone. Interest accrues through both the 3-month rehab and the 2-month sale period.

## Risks
- **Thin margin (caution flag):** Profit is under 10% of the resale price. A lower sale price, a rehab overrun, or a slow sale could erase most of the gain.
- **Resale price:** The whole return depends on reaching an after repair value of $232,000. The table gives no comparable sales to support it, so that figure should be checked independently.
- **Time:** The plan assumes 3 months of rehab and 2 months to sell. Any delay adds interest, property tax of $2,800 per year, and insurance of $1,400 per year.
- **Cost of capital:** An 11% rate plus 2% points is expensive money, and the 8% selling costs are large. Both take a meaningful share of the gross spread.
- **Rehab budget:** The $38,000 budget has no stated contingency. A flip with little margin has little tolerance for surprises behind the walls.

## Bottom line
The deal pencils out, but narrowly. The return percentages are high because of speed, not safety. I would proceed only with a verified resale value, a firm rehab bid, and cash reserves above the $26,586 peak. If the after repair value comes in weaker than assumed, the profit is at risk.
