The assignment fee is negative, so assigning at the contract price would be a loss, not a fee. Both the buyer's ceiling of $76,758 and the 70% rule maximum of $73,600 sit below what you have under contract.
- **Earnest money is exposed.** Your $1,000 is at risk if you cannot assign or renegotiate and then fail to close.
- **Rehab estimate carries weight.** At $65,000, the repair budget is a large part of the buyer's math. If the true cost runs higher, the buyer's ceiling falls further and the gap widens.
- **No cushion for a fee.** Any positive assignment fee requires the buyer to pay close to their limit, which leaves them little room for surprises and makes them harder to find.

## Bottom line
Do not try to assign this contract at $96,000. The price has to come down well below the buyer's maximum of $76,758 before there is room for a fee. If the seller will not reprice, use whatever exit the contract allows to limit the loss to the $1,000 earnest money. Recheck the rehab estimate before any renegotiation, since it drives the buyer's ceiling.
