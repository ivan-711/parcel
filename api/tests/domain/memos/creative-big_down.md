## Summary
This is a subject-to purchase of a fourplex at $310,000, with a $205,000 existing loan taken over at 3.2%, $45,000 cash to the seller, and a seller note at 4.5%. On the stated figures the deal holds up, with a 9.9% cap rate and a debt service coverage ratio of 2.00. The structure carries two real risks: the due-on-sale clause and the balloon in month 84.

## Returns
Stabilized net operating income is $30,680, and stabilized cash flow is $13,119 a year, or $1,093 a month. Cash needed at closing is $48,100, which is also the most cash in the deal at once. Cash on cash return is 27.3%, and annualized return on cash is 22.9%. Yield on total cost is 9.8%. Payback comes in 41 months.

Over the 120-month hold, total profit is $329,406, IRR is 39%, and the equity multiple is 4.34x. These results assume 3% rent growth and 3% appreciation per year, which gives a sale price of $416,614 and net proceeds of $245,925. If either assumption fails, the exit figures will fall short of this.

## Financing
The existing loan balance of $205,000 stays in place at 3.2%. The seller receives $45,000 in cash and holds a note at 4.5% with an 84-month balloon. Debt service coverage of 2.00 shows ample room for the payments on the stated rents. Break even occupancy is 60.8%, so occupancy can fall well below the 5% vacancy allowance before cash flow turns negative.

## Risks
1. **Due-on-sale exposure.** The seller's lender can call the existing loan due when the deed transfers. If that happens, the buyer would need to refinance or pay off the loan on short notice. The low 3.2% rate is the main source of the returns, and it would likely be lost in a refinance. This is the most serious risk because it is outside the buyer's control.
2. **Balloon payment.** A $52,216 balloon payment comes due in month 84. The plan needs a refinance, a sale, or cash on hand by then. The hold runs 120 months, so the balloon falls well before the planned exit.
3. **Growth assumptions.** The exit value depends on 3% annual appreciation and rent growth. Flat markets would reduce profit and the equity multiple.
4. **Expense estimates.** Reserves and management are set at percentages of rent. Actual repair costs on a fourplex can run uneven from year to year.

## Bottom line
The numbers are strong and leave a wide margin on coverage and occupancy. The deal's weak points are in the structure, not the operations. Proceed only if there is a plan for a loan call and a funded path to the month-84 balloon.
