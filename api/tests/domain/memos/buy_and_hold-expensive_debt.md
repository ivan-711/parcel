## Summary
This is a buy-and-hold purchase of a duplex at 1420 N 14th St, Sheboygan, WI for $218,000, financed with 25% down. The property-level numbers look fine, but the leveraged cash flow is thin and the loan coverage falls short of what most lenders want.

## Returns
Stabilized year net operating income is $17,757, a cap rate of 8.1% on the purchase price and a yield on total cost of 7.9%. After debt service, stabilized year cash flow is $1,332, or $111 per month. Cash on cash return is only 2.2%.

The bulk of the return comes from the exit, not from operations. Over the 120-month hold, total profit is $106,841, with an IRR of 12%, an equity multiple of 2.75x, and an annualized return on cash of 10.6%. Sale price at exit is $292,974, with net proceeds of $130,530. Payback comes at 120 months, meaning the cash is recovered only at the sale.

## Financing
Cash needed at closing is $61,040, which is also the most cash in the deal at once. The loan carries an 8.5% rate over 360 months. The debt service coverage ratio is 1.18, below the 1.25 many lenders require. Break even occupancy is 89.2%, so there is limited room for vacancy beyond the 5% allowance before cash flow turns negative.

## Risks
- **Coverage is below lender norms.** A DSCR of 1.18 may limit loan options or require more down payment.
- **Debt costs more than the property earns.** The 8.1% cap rate sits below the 8.5% loan rate, so leverage lowers the return rather than raising it.
- **Thin operating cushion.** Monthly cash flow of $111 and break even occupancy of 89.2% leave little margin for a vacancy spell, repair, or tax or insurance increase.
- **Reliance on growth assumptions.** The returns depend on 3% annual rent growth and 3% annual appreciation. If either falls short, the 12% IRR and 2.75x multiple will fall with it.
- **Illiquid payoff.** Most of the profit arrives at the sale in month 120.

## Bottom line
The property earns a reasonable yield on its own, but the financing as structured works against it. With coverage at 1.18 and cash on cash at 2.2%, this deal depends on appreciation and rent growth to reach its 12% IRR. I would want a lower rate, a larger down payment, or a lower price before proceeding.
