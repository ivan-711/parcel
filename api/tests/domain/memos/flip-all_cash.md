## Summary
This is a Cape Cod fix and flip at 915 Erie Ave, Sheboygan, WI. It is bought at $139,000, rehabbed for $38,000 over 3 months, and sold at an after repair value of $232,000. The numbers hold up on paper, but the profit depends on hitting the full ARV on schedule.

## Returns
- Total profit over the hold: $31,010
- IRR: 51.2%
- Equity multiple: 1.17x
- Annualized return on cash: 45.9%
- Payback: 5 months
- Net proceeds from sale: $213,440, after selling costs of 8%

The IRR and annualized return look strong because the hold is short. The equity multiple of 1.17x is the better measure of what the project earns on the cash put in. It is a modest return for the effort and the risk.

## Risks
No flags were raised. The figures still show these concerns:

- **No pricing cushion.** The sale price at exit is $232,000, the same as the after repair value. Any shortfall at sale comes straight out of the $31,010 profit.
- **Selling costs.** At 8%, they take a large share of the gross sale price. A lower price or a negotiated concession would cut into profit quickly.
- **Capital at risk.** The most cash in the deal at once is $181,900, well above the $141,780 needed at closing. The difference is rehab and carrying costs, so a rehab overrun adds to an already large cash position.
- **Timeline.** The 5-month payback assumes 3 months of rehab and 2 months to sell. Delays add property tax of $2,800 per year and insurance of $1,400 per year, and they lower the annualized figures that make this deal look attractive.
- **Single exit.** The return depends on one sale at the projected value. The table has no rental or refinance fallback.

## Bottom line
The deal works at the stated assumptions, with a profit of $31,010 and a 5-month payback. The cushion is thin, because the exit price equals the ARV and selling costs are 8%. I would want the $232,000 ARV supported by recent comparable sales, and a rehab contingency on top of the $38,000 budget, before committing roughly $181,900 at peak.
