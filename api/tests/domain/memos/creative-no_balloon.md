## Summary
This is a subject-to purchase of a fourplex at 3305 S 12th St, Sheboygan, WI, for $310,000. The buyer takes over an existing $205,000 loan at 3.2%, pays $15,000 cash to the seller, and signs a seller note at 4.5%. The numbers hold up on paper, but they depend on the existing loan staying in place.

## Returns
- Stabilized net operating income is $30,680. Cap rate on purchase price is 9.9%, and yield on total cost is 9.8%.
- Stabilized cash flow is $11,295 a year, or $941 a month.
- Cash needed at closing is $18,100, which is also the most cash in the deal at once. Cash on cash return is 62.4% and annualized return on cash is 33.6%.
- Over the 120-month hold, total profit is $310,356, IRR is 94.3%, and the equity multiple is 18.15x. Payback is 19 months.
- Exit sale price is $416,614, with net proceeds of $173,844.

These return figures are very high because so little cash goes in. They show how thin the equity is as much as how strong the property is.

## Financing
- The buyer assumes $205,000 at 3.2%, pays $15,000 to the seller, and carries a seller note at 4.5%.
- Debt service coverage is 1.79. Break even occupancy is 65.6%, which leaves room against the 5% vacancy allowance.
- The table does not give the seller note's balance, term or balloon date. Those terms should be pinned down before closing.

## Risks
1. **Due-on-sale.** The seller's lender can call the existing loan due when the deed transfers. The flag is marked info, but it is the main risk. The low 3.2% rate drives the cash flow. If the loan is called, the buyer must refinance at current rates or lose the property, and the returns above would no longer apply.
2. **Thin cash cushion.** With $18,100 at closing, the buyer has little reserve beyond the 5% maintenance and 5% capital allowances.
3. **Growth assumptions.** The exit price of $416,614 relies on 3% annual appreciation, and the income projection relies on 3% annual rent growth. Neither is guaranteed.
4. **Seller note terms.** Unknown payment structure and maturity could strain cash flow or force a refinance.
5. **Title and insurance.** The seller's name stays on the loan, which complicates insurance and any later sale.

## Bottom line
The property performs well on the stated figures, and coverage of 1.79 is comfortable. The deal is only as sound as the existing loan's survival. I would not proceed without a plan to refinance if the lender calls the loan, and I would want the seller note terms in writing.
