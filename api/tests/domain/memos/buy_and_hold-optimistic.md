## Summary
This is a buy-and-hold purchase of a duplex at 1420 N 14th St, Sheboygan, WI, for $218,000 with 25% down. The headline numbers are strong: an 8.5% cap rate, a debt service coverage ratio of 1.45 and an 18.6% IRR. Several inputs are thin, though, and they flatter the results.

## Returns
Stabilized year net operating income is $18,493, and stabilized year cash flow is $5,768, or $481 per month. Cash on cash return is 9.4%, and yield on total cost is 8.2%. Over the 120-month hold, total profit is $159,638 on $61,040 of cash invested, an equity multiple of 3.62x and an annualized return on cash of 13.7%. Payback comes at 95 months. The exit assumes a sale price of $292,974 and net proceeds of $135,928. A large share of the profit depends on 3% annual appreciation and 3% annual rent growth holding for the whole hold.

## Financing
The loan carries a 6.8% rate over 360 months. Cash needed at closing is $61,040, which is also the most cash in the deal at once. A debt service coverage ratio of 1.45 is adequate. Break even occupancy is 74.5%, which leaves a reasonable cushion on paper.

## Risks
- **Vacancy is set at 2%.** That is optimistic for most markets. A duplex has very few units, so one empty unit removes a large share of rent, far more than the allowance. That one vacancy would likely push occupancy below the 74.5% break even.
- **Capital reserve is 0%.** Nothing is set aside for roofs, furnaces and other big replacements. The 5% maintenance reserve covers routine repairs, not these. A major replacement would come straight out of the $481 monthly cash flow or the owner's pocket.
- **Thin cash flow.** Stabilized year cash flow of $5,768 is a small margin for a building with no capital reserve and a loan at 6.8%.
- **Growth assumptions.** The exit value and much of the profit rest on steady 3% rent growth and 3% appreciation.

## Bottom line
The deal looks sound on paper, but the numbers are better than a typical duplex would deliver. Rerun it with a higher vacancy allowance and a real capital reserve before relying on the 18.6% IRR. If it still covers debt service with room to spare under those inputs, it is worth pursuing.
