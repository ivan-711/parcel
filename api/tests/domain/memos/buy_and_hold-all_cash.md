## Summary
This is an all-cash purchase of a duplex at 1420 N 14th St, Sheboygan, for $218,000, held for 120 months. The numbers hold up: a cap rate of 8.1% and an IRR of 10%, with no flags raised.

## Returns
Stabilized year net operating income is $17,757, which gives a cap rate on purchase price of 8.1% and a yield on total cost of 7.9%. Stabilized year cash flow is $16,418, or $1,368 per month, for a cash on cash return of 7.3%. Annualized return on cash is 7.5%.

Over the hold, total profit is $239,067 on $224,540 of cash at closing, an equity multiple of 2.06x and an IRR of 10%. The sale at exit is projected at $292,974, with net proceeds of $275,395. Payback comes at 120 months, so the cash is returned only at the sale.

## Risks
No flags were raised. The figures still show a few things to watch:
- **Payback depends on the sale.** Payback lands at month 120, so the return of capital relies on exit pricing. The exit assumes 3% appreciation per year, and a flat market would cut into the profit.
- **Rent growth is assumed.** The model uses 3% rent growth per year. Slower growth would lower cash flow and the exit value.
- **All cash is tied up in one asset.** The most cash in the deal at once is $224,540, with no leverage and no diversification.
- **Reserves are thin on a small property.** Maintenance and capital reserves are each 5%. Older duplexes can need large one-time repairs, and the table gives no property age or condition. Confirm both before closing.
- **Cushion is wide.** Break even occupancy is 24%, so the rent roll could fall a long way before cash flow turns negative. Vacancy is allowed at 5%, which is modest but well covered.

## Bottom line
The deal works as an unlevered hold. Income is steady and the break even is low. The 10% IRR relies on appreciation and rent growth at 3% each, so test those assumptions and inspect the building's condition before committing $224,540.
