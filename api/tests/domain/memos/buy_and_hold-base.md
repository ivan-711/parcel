## Summary
This is a buy-and-hold purchase of the duplex at 1420 N 14th St, Sheboygan, for $218,000, with rent of $2,350 a month. The numbers hold up. Income covers the debt with room to spare, but most of the return comes from the sale at the end of the hold.

## Returns
- IRR is 15.5%, equity multiple is 3.23x, and annualized return on cash is 12.4%.
- Total profit over the hold is $135,845 on $61,040 of cash invested.
- Stabilized year net operating income is $17,757, a cap rate of 8.1% on the purchase price. Yield on total cost is 7.9%.
- Stabilized year cash flow is $3,692, or $308 a month. Cash on cash return is 6%.
- Exit sale price is $292,974, with net proceeds of $135,928.

## Financing
- The down payment is 25%, and the loan runs 360 months at 6.8%.
- Cash needed at closing is $61,040, which is also the most cash in the deal at once.
- Debt service coverage ratio is 1.40. Break even occupancy is 79%.

## Risks
No flags were raised. The figures still show the following:
- **Exit dependence.** Cash on cash is 6%, while IRR is 15.5%. Payback comes at 120 months, the same as the hold period, so the invested cash is not recovered until the sale.
- **Growth assumptions.** The model assumes 3% rent growth and 3% appreciation per year. If either comes in lower, the sale proceeds and the IRR fall.
- **Thin monthly cushion.** Cash flow of $308 a month leaves little room for a large repair or an extended vacancy, even with the reserves built in.
- **Modest spread over the loan rate.** The 8.1% cap rate sits above the 6.8% loan rate, but not by a wide margin. A higher rate on any refinance would narrow it.
- **Expense assumptions.** Management at 8%, maintenance at 5% and capital reserve at 5% are reasonable, but they should be checked against actual quotes and the building's condition.

## Bottom line
The deal works on paper. Coverage of 1.40 and break even occupancy of 79% against a 5% vacancy allowance give a reasonable margin. Current cash flow is modest, and the strong IRR depends on growth and a sale at $292,974. I would proceed only after confirming the rent, the tax and insurance figures, and the building's condition.
