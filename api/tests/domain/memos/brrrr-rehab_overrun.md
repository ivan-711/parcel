## Summary
This is a BRRRR on a ranch at 2210 Kohler Ave: $112,000 purchase, $70,000 rehab over 4 months, and an after repair value of $205,000. The numbers do not hold up as a rental. The refinance does not clear the first loan, and the stabilized year runs at a loss. The return depends on the sale at exit.

## Returns
Over the 120-month hold, total profit is $92,116, with an IRR of 12.7% and an equity multiple of 3.08x. Annualized return on cash is 11.9%. Payback takes the full 120 months, so the cash comes back only at sale.

Stabilized net operating income is $13,151, a 11.7% cap rate on purchase price. Yield on total cost is 7.1%. After debt service, stabilized cash flow is -$197 for the year, or -$16 a month, and cash on cash is -0.4%. The exit sale price is $272,802, with net proceeds of $122,349. That price rests on 3% yearly appreciation holding for the full hold.

## Financing
The purchase loan is 85% of cost at 11%. The refinance comes after 6 months at 75% loan to value and a 7% rate. Cash needed at closing is $22,064, and the most cash in the deal at once is $44,369. After the refinance, $44,163 is still in the deal, so almost none of the cash comes back out. That is not what a BRRRR is meant to do.

## Risks
- **Refinance shortfall.** The new loan falls $13,781 short of paying off the first loan. You must cover that from your own cash. If the appraisal comes in under $205,000, the gap grows.
- **Negative cash flow.** The stabilized year loses money every month. Any rise in repairs, vacancy or taxes deepens the loss.
- **Thin coverage.** The debt service coverage ratio is 1.07. Most lenders want 1.25 or better, so the refinance may be hard to place on these terms.
- **Little room on occupancy.** Break even occupancy is 96.1%, against a 5% vacancy allowance in the model.
- **Expensive bridge debt.** The 11% purchase loan runs through the rehab and the wait for the refinance. Delays raise its cost.

## Bottom line
I would not approve this as structured. The headline IRR comes from appreciation and the sale, not from rental income. The deal needs a lower purchase or rehab cost, higher rent, or a larger refinance before it works as a hold.
