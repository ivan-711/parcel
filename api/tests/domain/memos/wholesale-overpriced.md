## Summary
This is a wholesale assignment of 1104 Michigan Ave, Sheboygan, WI, under contract at $135,000. The numbers do not hold up. A flipper who needs a 15% margin can pay at most $98,586, which is below what you have committed to pay.

## Returns
- After repair value is $198,000 and the rehab estimate is $42,000.
- The most a buyer can pay is $98,586. At that price the buyer's profit is $29,700.
- Your assignment fee is -$36,414, so assigning at the buyer's ceiling means a loss, not a fee.
- The 70% rule maximum offer is $96,600, which is also well under your contract price.

## Risks
1. **Contract price is above what a flipper can pay.** This is the flagged risk and the deal-breaker. Your $135,000 price exceeds the buyer's $98,586 ceiling, and no fee is available at that price.
2. **Negative assignment fee.** The -$36,414 figure means you would have to cover the gap yourself or close the deal at a loss.
3. **Earnest money.** You have $1,000 at risk if you cannot assign the contract or close it.
4. **Buyer cash needs.** The buyer needs $13,605 in cash at closing. That is manageable, but it does not fix the price problem.
5. **Seller dependence.** The deal only works if the estate will accept a lower price, and the figures give no sign that it will.

## Bottom line
Do not try to market this contract at $135,000. Go back to the estate and renegotiate toward the buyer's ceiling of $98,586, and lower still if you want a fee. The 70% rule maximum of $96,600 is a reasonable reference point. If the seller will not move, the better choice is to cancel while the loss is limited to the $1,000 earnest money, provided the contract allows it.
