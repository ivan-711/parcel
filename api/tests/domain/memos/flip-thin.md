## Summary
This is a fix and flip of a Cape Cod house at 915 Erie Ave, Sheboygan, WI. The plan is a purchase at $139,000, a $38,000 rehab over 3 months, and a sale at $195,000 after 2 months on the market. The numbers do not hold up: the deal loses money.

## Returns
- Total profit over the hold: -$12,311
- IRR: -83.5%
- Equity multiple: 0.54x
- Annualized return on cash: -77.5%
- Net proceeds from sale: $16,300

The sale price at exit is $195,000, equal to the after repair value. Net proceeds of $16,300 do not return the cash put in, which is why the equity multiple is 0.54x.

## Financing
The loan covers 90% of cost at 11% interest with 2% points. Cash needed at closing is $19,182. The most cash in the deal at once is $26,586, so the investor must fund more than the closing amount before the sale. Interest and points weigh heavily on a hold of 3 months of rehab plus 2 months to sell.

## Risks
- **Profit is negative, not just thin.** The caution flag says profit is under 10% of the resale price and leaves little room for surprises. The figures show a loss of $12,311, so there is no margin to protect.
- **Selling costs of 8%** take a large share of a $195,000 sale.
- **Financing costs.** The 11% rate and 2% points add to a project that already runs at a loss.
- **Holding costs.** Property tax of $2,800 per year and insurance of $1,400 per year run through the whole rehab and sale period.
- **No cushion on value.** The exit price equals the after repair value. A lower sale price deepens the loss.
- **Timing.** A longer rehab or a slower sale adds interest, tax and insurance to a deal that is already underwater.
- **Rehab overruns.** The $38,000 budget has no visible contingency, and any overrun adds to the loss and to the cash needed.

## Bottom line
I would not approve this deal as structured. The loss of $12,311 and the 0.54x equity multiple show the purchase price, rehab scope and resale value do not work together. It would need a lower purchase price, a smaller rehab budget, or solid comparable sales supporting a higher resale value before I would look at it again.
