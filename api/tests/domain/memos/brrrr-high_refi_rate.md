## Summary
This is a BRRRR on a ranch at 2210 Kohler Ave, Sheboygan, WI: buy at $112,000, spend $46,000 on rehab over 4 months, and refinance against an after repair value of $205,000. The headline return is strong, with an IRR of 18.9%, but the stabilized rental does not carry its own debt. The numbers only hold up if the investor funds the shortfall and relies on sale proceeds at exit.

## Returns
Total profit over the hold is $102,020, an equity multiple of 4.09x and an annualized return on cash of 16.2%. Nearly all of that comes from the exit: sale price of $272,802 and net proceeds of $119,542, which rest on 3% annual appreciation over 120 months. Payback is 120 months, so cash is not recovered before the sale. Stabilized net operating income is $13,151, a cap rate of 11.7% on purchase price and a yield on total cost of 8.2%.

## Financing
The purchase loan is 85% of cost at 11%. The refinance comes after 6 months at 75% loan to value and an 8% rate. Cash needed at closing is $22,064, and the most cash in the deal at once is $29,315. The refinance returns $10,219, which leaves $19,393 in the deal. This is not a full cash-out; a large share of the investor's capital stays tied up.

## Risks
- **Debt coverage is below 1.** The debt service coverage ratio is 0.97, so net operating income does not cover the loan payments.
- **Negative cash flow.** The stabilized year shows -$1,466, or -$122 a month, with a cash on cash return of -7.6%. The investor pays out of pocket every month of the hold.
- **No occupancy cushion.** Break even occupancy is 103.4%, which is above full occupancy. Even a fully rented property does not break even.
- **Capital left in.** $19,393 stays in the deal after the refinance, so the BRRRR goal of recycling capital is only partly met.
- **Exit dependence.** The 18.9% IRR depends on appreciation and rent growth of 3% a year each. Flat values would erase much of the profit while the property keeps bleeding cash.
- **Rehab risk.** A budget overrun or delay beyond 4 months would raise the cost basis and push the refinance back, extending time at the 11% purchase rate.

## Bottom line
The return looks good on paper, but it is built on the sale, not on operations. The property loses money each month and fails to cover its debt. I would not proceed unless the price, the rent or the refinance terms improve enough to bring debt coverage above 1, or the investor is willing to fund ongoing losses for the full hold.
